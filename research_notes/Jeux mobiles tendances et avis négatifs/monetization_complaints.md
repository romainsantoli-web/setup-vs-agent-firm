# Monetization complaints in negative reviews of top mobile games (2024-2026)

> ⚠️ Contenu généré par IA — validation humaine requise avant utilisation.
>
> Method note: research done on 2026-10-22 with web search only. Direct page fetching was blocked by the session's network proxy (appfollow.io, dl.acm.org, unstar.app, sportskeeda.com all refused), so the quotes and figures below come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages. I could not open the full pages to check them. Short quotes are reproduced as the search tool extracted them. Treat exact wording as "near-verbatim, to verify". Several sources are vendor blogs or complaint aggregators (Kimola, Sikayetvar, Unstar, AppFollow, Tapjoy, Unity), and each is flagged where it appears.

## Q1. What are the most frequent monetization complaints?

### Takeaway
Across vendor review-mining, academic dark-pattern studies and complaint boards, the recurring complaint clusters are:
1. **Pay-to-win and power for sale** (strongest in competitive titles and 4X)
2. **Intrusive or forced ads**, including ads that persist after a paid "remove ads" (casual and hyper-casual)
3. **Engineered difficulty and "pay to continue" with lives/energy** (puzzle titles)
4. **Random-reward and gacha pressure**: pack luck, duplicates, too few free pulls
5. **Price opacity through premium currencies and bundles**

Players generally accept cosmetic purchases and opt-in rewarded ads.

### Cited Findings
- **Share of monetization in negative reviews (vendor estimates, unverified):** Unstar, a vendor of review-monitoring tools, claims that "aggressive monetization and pay-to-win" account for **34% of negative reviews** of mobile games. It gives **41%** for casual and puzzle games with ads. It discloses no dataset, sample size or method. — [Unstar, "Gaming app reviews: what mobile gamers hate most 2026"](https://unstar.app/blog/gaming-app-reviews-what-mobile-gamers-hate-most-2026)
- AppFollow's sample table shows **monetization at 24% of negative reviews** (about 900 reviews in the example). It gives a rule of thumb: if more than **20-25%** of a game's reviews mention pricing or ads negatively, its monetization is "probably too aggressive". (Vendor guidance, illustrative sample.) — [AppFollow, "Monetization & paywall complaints: a game studio playbook"](https://appfollow.io/blog/monetization-paywall-complaints-mobile-game-reviews)
- AppFollow ranks **forced ads as the #1 monetization complaint**. It says players "rarely object to ads themselves", object to interruptions instead, and complain mostly about bad placement such as ads covering buttons. — [AppFollow, "Mobile Game Monetization 2026: The Expert Playbook"](https://appfollow.io/blog/mobile-game-monetization); [AppFollow, "Mobile Game Ads: 7 Ad Formats"](https://appfollow.io/blog/mobile-game-ads-formats-monetization)
- AppFollow on pay-to-win: players buy cosmetics but resent paying for power, because power purchases affect competitive balance. Its advice is to "monetize expression before advantage". Limited-time battle passes draw complaints of manipulation through FOMO. — [AppFollow playbook](https://appfollow.io/blog/mobile-game-monetization)
- **Academic, review-based:** "Level Up or Game Over: Exploring How Dark Patterns Shape Mobile Games" (ACM, MUM 2024) analysed user-reported dark patterns for **1,496 iOS and Android games** from darkpatterns.games.
  - It uses four categories: temporal, monetary, social and psychological.
  - Only about **10% of games had no reported dark pattern**.
  - **442 (52.4%) of the "dark" games carried advertisements**, which suggests a link between dark patterns and ad monetization.
  - [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3701571.3701604); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386555504_Level_Up_or_Game_Over_Exploring_How_Dark_Patterns_Shape_Mobile_Games)
- **Academic, survey-based (2025):** in "Dark Patterns in Games: An Empirical Study of Their Harmfulness" (Veiga et al., SciTePress 2025):
  - **Monetary dark patterns** such as loot boxes and "invested value" were the most widely recognised.
  - The monetary category was rated the **most harmful**.
  - **Paywalls had the highest perceived harm.**
  - [SciTePress PDF](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2025/133658/133658.pdf)
- **Academic, app-store reviews:** Sameen & Rashid (University of Bristol, IEEE ConPro 2024) manually analysed **500 Google Play and App Store reviews** of **34 trending games**. They identified **35 types of dark patterns**. Per-pattern percentages were not visible in the search extract. — [ConPro'24 PDF](https://ieee-security.org/TC/SPW2024/ConPro/papers/sameen-conpro24.pdf)
- **Industry review study (2024 data, published around 2025):** Mobile Premier League analysed the **75,000 most relevant Google Play reviews** of the top 5 most-downloaded games, using AppRadar's 2024 ranking.
  - **Candy Crush Saga** showed "a huge spike in negative sentiment", with many reviews "specifically calling out intrusive ads".
  - Free Fire showed negative sentiment toward its updates.
  - Subway Surfers reviews were mostly positive about updates.
  - [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/players-rebuke-clumsy-ad-strategies-even-in-popular-games-mobile-premier-league/)
- **Ads after paying "remove ads":**
  - A Google Play review listing from **September 2025** reads "too many ads even after paying $5 to remove ads". This was surfaced via the review aggregator marlvel.ai; I could not confirm which game it refers to. — [marlvel.ai reviews page](https://marlvel.ai/apps/fill-one-line-puzzle-game/reviews)
  - SEGA keeps a support article titled "I have previously paid to remove adverts but I am still seeing them in my game", which shows the issue is common enough to need a standard FAQ. — [SEGA Support](https://support.sega.com/hc/en-gb/articles/39572820877713-I-have-previously-paid-to-remove-adverts-but-I-am-still-seeing-them-in-my-game-What-should-I-do)
  - Older precedents, not 2024-2026: Glu (Space Monkey) and Gameloft (Hero of Sparta 2) removed ads they had added to already-paid games after backlash. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/10293/glu-removes-ads-from-iphone-space-monkey/); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=20666)

### Inferences
- Ranked by how often the sources mention them:
  1. Pay-to-win and power for sale
  2. Ads (frequency, unskippable, placement, ads after "remove ads")
  3. Difficulty walls monetized through lives, extra moves or boosters
  4. Random-reward frustration (gacha, packs, duplicates)
  5. Price opacity through currencies and bundles
  6. Battle-pass and time-limited FOMO
- This ranking is qualitative. No independent source gives a robust percentage split.
- The 24-41% range for "share of negative reviews tied to monetization" comes only from vendors with undisclosed methods. A new game should treat it as an order of magnitude, roughly a quarter to a third of 1-2 star reviews, not as a benchmark.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed 2024-2026 paper that gives a percentage breakdown of *monetization* topics within 1-2 star mobile game reviews. The academic work measures dark patterns, which overlap with monetization but are not the same thing.
- I found no Sensor Tower, AppTweak or GameRefinery review-sentiment study on monetization share in this search.
- Subscription traps (auto-renew VIP passes) and "misleading offers" (for example a "best value" label on worse value) showed up only in regulatory documents (see Q4), not in quantified review data.

## Q2. Which top games draw these complaints, and how severe are they?

### Takeaway
- **Most severe and best documented:**
  - Clash Royale: evolutions and tower troops locked behind passes or $50-100 offers
  - Pokémon TCG Pocket: trade-token backlash serious enough to force a reversal
  - Genshin Impact: a 2026 review-bombing over free pulls
  - Royal Match: engineered "one move short" difficulty
  - Monopoly GO: dice scarcity and "rigged rolls" suspicion
  - EA FC Mobile: pack gambling and market prices
- **Next tier, mostly forum and complaint-board evidence:** Whiteout Survival and Last War (whale balance, rollout speed), Brawl Stars (prices), Candy Crush (intrusive ads).

### Cited Findings
**Clash Royale (Supercell)**
- 2025 coverage says unlocking the first four card **evolutions** without spending seemed "nearly impossible" unless a player paid a **$100 special offer**.
- Monthly evolutions moved behind an **$11.99 monthly pass**.
- **Tower troops** such as the Cannoneer were first locked behind **$50 offers**, which created "a clear gap between spenders and free players".

**Brawl Stars (Supercell)**

The change was scheduled for autumn 2025. — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/143482/pokemon-tcg-pocket-trade-tokens-update); [esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/pokemon-tcg-pocket/pokemon-tcg-pocket-to-remove-trade-tokens-and-require-shinedust-for-trades); [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/pokemon-tcg-pocket/trade-update)

### Inferences
- Severity tiers for a design brief:
  - **Tier 1, forced company reversals or protests:** Pokémon TCG Pocket (trade tokens reversed), Genshin Impact (review-bombing), Whiteout Survival (boycott with partial reversal).
  - **Tier 2, persistent "P2W" label in reviews and press:** Clash Royale, EA FC Mobile, Monopoly GO, Royal Match.
  - **Tier 3, ad-annoyance complaints with otherwise positive sentiment:** Subway Surfers, Candy Crush.
- **Design lesson:** a reversal follows when monetization is attached to a *social or core-loop* feature that players see as basic, such as trading or free pulls. Monetizing extras draws less backlash.

### Gaps
- No usable evidence for Royal Kingdom, Roblox, Call of Duty Mobile or Free Fire beyond MPL's general note on Free Fire.
- No Reddit threads came up in searches; the search engine returned mostly aggregators.
- No quantified per-game share of 1-2 star reviews about monetization. Kimola reports exist, but their percentages were not visible.

## Q3. Studies and analyses quantifying monetization in negative reviews

### Takeaway
Rigorous quantification is scarce.
- **Academic work** measures dark patterns: about 90% of 1,496 mobile games have at least one user-reported dark pattern, and monetary patterns are rated most harmful.
- **Vendor work** (AppFollow, Unstar) puts monetization at roughly 24-41% of negative reviews, with undisclosed methods.
- **The free-to-play spending base is tiny:** roughly 2-3% of players pay. This explains how aggressively games convert the rest.

### Cited Findings
- About 10% of 1,496 mobile games had no reported dark pattern, and 52.4% of "dark" games used ads. — [ACM MUM'24](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3701571.3701604)
- Monetary dark patterns are the most recognised and the most harmful; paywalls are rated most harmful of all. — [SciTePress 2025](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2025/133658/133658.pdf)
- 500 reviews of 34 trending games yielded 35 dark-pattern types. — [Sameen & Rashid, ConPro'24](https://ieee-security.org/TC/SPW2024/ConPro/papers/sameen-conpro24.pdf)
- Monetization makes up 24% of negative reviews in AppFollow's example; above 20-25% means "too aggressive". — [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/monetization-paywall-complaints-mobile-game-reviews)
- "Aggressive monetization and pay-to-win" make up 34% of negative reviews, and 41% for casual and puzzle games with ads (vendor figure, no method disclosed). — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/gaming-app-reviews-what-mobile-gamers-hate-most-2026)
- MPL's study of 75,000 reviews of the top 5 Google Play games found that download volume "doesn't necessarily correlate with player satisfaction". — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/players-rebuke-clumsy-ad-strategies-even-in-popular-games-mobile-premier-league/)
- Older ad-review research (flagged as 2017): "What Aspects of Mobile Ads Do Users Care About? An Empirical Study of Mobile In-app Ad Reviews". — [arXiv 1702.07681](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.07681)
- Unity says only about **3% of users** make in-app purchases, which explains the heavy reliance on ads. — [Unity glossary (FR)](https://unity.com/fr/glossary/mobile-game-business-model)
- French press reports that fewer than 2 in 100 players spend money in freemium games (older article). — [LesMobiles.com](https://www.lesmobiles.com/actualite/13413-moins-de-2-joueurs-sur-100-depensent-de-l-argent-dans-un-jeu-freemium.html)

### Inferences
- For a new game, a practical KPI is to keep monetization mentions below roughly 20% of 1-2 star reviews, using AppFollow's heuristic. Track them by sub-topic: P2W, ads, energy, prices and gacha.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 peer-reviewed review-mining paper specific to mobile game *monetization* was found. An arXiv or alphaXiv deep search could close this gap.
- Sensor Tower, AppTweak and GameRefinery sentiment studies were not found.

## Q4. Regulatory context (EU, loot boxes, store policies)

### Takeaway
EU enforcement on in-game currencies has escalated:
1. **September 2024:** BEUC complaint.
2. **March 2025:** CPC "Key Principles" on in-game virtual currencies.
3. **September 30, 2026:** coordinated CPC action against about 10 publishers, including King and Supercell.

A Digital Fairness Act that could ban loot boxes for minors is expected around Q4 2026. Loot-box rules vary by country:
- **Belgium and the Netherlands:** treated as gambling or banned.
- **UK:** from June 2026, PEGI 16+ for new games with paid loot boxes, plus ASA disclosure rules.
- **Brazil:** ban on sales to under-18s reported from March 2026.
- **Google Play and Apple:** must disclose odds before purchase.

### Cited Findings
- **BEUC complaint (12 September 2024):**
  - BEUC and **22 member organisations from 17 countries** complained to the Commission and the CPC network.
  - Targets include Activision Blizzard, EA, Epic, Mojang, Roblox, Supercell and Ubisoft, over games such as Fortnite, EA FC 24, Minecraft and Clash of Clans.
  - Allegations: premium currencies obscure real prices, and bundles force overspend.
  - Under-18s spend on average **€39 per month** on in-game purchases.
  - BEUC asks for a **ban on paid in-game currencies**, in-app purchases factored into age ratings, and price transparency.
  - [BEUC press release](https://www.beuc.eu/press-releases/consumer-groups-denounce-video-games-manipulative-spending-tactics); [BEUC report "Game over"](https://beuc.eu/reports/game-over-consumers-fight-fairer-game-purchases); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/eu-consumer-organisation-files-complaint-saying-devs-are-fooling-consumers-with-in-game-currencies); [Sveriges Konsumenter legal assessment PDF](https://sverigeskonsumenter.se/media/1q2iahn1/gameover_alegalassessmentofpremiumin-gamecurrenciesfromaconsumerlawperspective.pdf)
- **CPC Key Principles (21 March 2025):**
  - Clear real-money pricing and no hidden costs.
  - Withdrawal rights respected.
  - Protection of vulnerable consumers and children.
  - Industry bodies VGE and EGDF called them "misinterpretations of EU consumer law".
  - [Forbrukertilsynet](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/eng-articles/national-consumer-authorities-and-commission-take-action-on-harmful-practices-in-the-gaming-industry); [Linklaters](https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102k6t4/game-changer-eu-introduces-consumer-protection-guidance-for-in-game-virtual-curr); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/global/regulatory-spotlight-virtual-currency-vs-consumer-law-part-2)
- **Star Stable Online (March 2025):** after a complaint from the Swedish Consumers' Association, the CPC found the game:
  - lacked clear, child-appropriate information on buying and using its currency
  - appealed directly to children
  - used time-limited pressure offers.
  - The company got one month to commit to changes. **No final outcome was found.** — [GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/en/business/case-of-star-stable-online-conflict-ahead-eu-commission-and-cpc-set-sights-on-games-industry-1870db4b2004d7d284e1c833a28c1f38); [European Commission coordinated actions](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/consumers/consumer-rights-and-complaints/enforcement-consumer-protection/coordinated-actions/social-media-online-games-and-search-engines_en)
- **CPC coordinated action (30 September 2026):**
  - Publishers: Activision Blizzard, Crytek, InnoGames, **King**, Mojang, Plarium, PLR Worldwide Sales, Riot, **Supercell** and Ubisoft.
  - Games: **Candy Crush Saga**, Minecraft, **Gardenscapes**, **Clash of Clans** and others.
  - Regulators found "a high number" of companies had made no substantive changes since March 2025.
  - Focus: loot boxes and variable rewards, dark patterns, and direct exhortation of children.
  - Enforceability is unclear because the principles are non-binding.
  - VGE and EGDF pushed back the same day.
  - The date is September 30 per PocketGamer.biz and mobilegamer.biz; CryptoRank says October 1.
  - [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/europes-cpc-targets-king-supercell-mojang-and-others-over-virtual-currencies/); [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/video-games-europe-hits-back-at-eu-watchdog-targeting-king-supercell-mojang-and-more-over-iap-practices/); [ICPEN](https://www.icpen.org/news/1508)
- **Digital Fairness Act:**
  - Still a proposal; the Commission work programme says tabling in 2026, possibly Q4.
  - BEUC (December 2025 position paper) wants paid currencies and paid loot boxes banned.
  - The European Parliament (October 2025) called for a default ban on the most harmful addictive practices for minors.
  - Lawyers expect either a loot-box ban or parental consent for minors.
  - [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/the-eus-proposed-digital-fairness-act-a-game-developers-guide-to-potential-imp-102ltio); [BEUC DFA paper PDF](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/BEUC-X-2025-110_Towards_the_Digital_Fairness_Act.pdf); [European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251013IPR30892/new-eu-measures-needed-to-make-online-services-safer-for-minors); [Wikipedia DFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Fairness_Act)
- **Loot boxes by country (mostly secondary compliance blogs; verify against official texts):**
  - **Belgium and the Netherlands:** paid loot boxes treated as illegal gambling. Note: a 2022 Dutch Council of State ruling overturned the EA fine, so the Dutch position is contested; this is from background knowledge and not verified in this session.
  - **UK:** not gambling under the 2005 Act. **PEGI 16 minimum for new games with paid loot boxes from June 2026.** An **ASA notice dated 26 February 2026** requires prominent loot-box disclosure in store listings and ads, with monitoring from 26 May 2026.

- **France, older background:** games with in-app purchases may no longer be labelled "gratuit" (CPC action, 2014). — [Phonandroid](https://www.phonandroid.com/?p=125230)

### Inferences
- A game launching in the EU in 2027 should design as if the CPC principles were binding:
  - show real-money prices next to currency prices
  - avoid currency pack sizes that leave unusable remainders
  - no countdown pressure aimed at minors
  - disclose odds, or avoid paid random rewards entirely
- This lowers both regulatory risk and the matching review complaints, such as price opacity and "casino" accusations.

## Q5. What players say they would prefer

### Takeaway
Players consistently signal four preferences:
- **Opt-in rewarded ads** over forced interstitials (majority preference in several vendor surveys).
- **Cosmetic-only or expression monetization** over buying power.
- **A meaningful earnable path** to premium items.
- **Fair, generous free rewards in gacha**, judged against competitors (Genshin vs. HSR and Wuthering Waves).

Evidence for "one-time purchase" preference is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- Tapjoy survey: **54%** of US consumers prefer opt-in rewarded ads, against **32%** for mandatory ads and **14%** for paid downloads. A related Tapjoy figure is **76% vs 24%** in favour of opt-in over mandatory ads. The surveys are undated (likely pre-2024; flagged) and vendor-run. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/302685/mobile-gamers-prefer-rewarded-video-ads.html); [AppDeveloperMagazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/reward-videos-for-mobile-ads-payoff-the-best-says-tapjoy/amp/)
- Unity survey: **46%** prefer incentivised video, against 29% for full-screen pop-ups, 20% for banners and 5% for standard interstitials. Older and vendor-run. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/unity-the-majority-of-mobile-gamers-are-open-to-engaging-with-incentivized-video-ads/)
- Monetizr (October 2023, about 3,000 respondents): **49.28%** said ad placement did not significantly affect their experience. Older and vendor-run. — [Mobile Marketing Magazine](https://mobilemarketingmagazine.com/study-reveals-gamers-attitudes-to-in-game-advertising)
- AppFollow's review analysis:
  - When in-app purchases are limited to cosmetics that do not affect gameplay, "players generally don't complain".
  - Players accept cosmetics taking longer to earn if a free path exists.
  - Battle-pass value is judged by whether rewards are achievable.
  - [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/what-mobile-game-players-want-monetization-insights-from-app-store-reviews)
- Players compare free-pull generosity across gacha games and punish the stingiest (Genshin 6.6 vs HSR and Wuthering Waves). — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/genshin-impact/genshin-impact-players-review-bomb-hoyoverse-office-on-google-maps-over-reward-backlash-3364172/)
- Pokémon TCG Pocket players demanded that trading use earnable resources (duplicates) rather than paid tokens, and the developers complied. — [esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/pokemon-tcg-pocket/pokemon-tcg-pocket-to-remove-trade-tokens-and-require-shinedust-for-trades)
- Royal Match draws praise for being largely ad-free, even from reviewers who criticise its difficulty. — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/candy-crush-royal-match-gardenscapes-homescapes-toon-blast-puzzle-games-ranked-2026)
- A Whiteout Survival petition asks for **phased rollouts** so free-to-play players are not instantly outclassed by new paid power. — [Change.org](https://www.change.org/p/stop-unbalanced-updates-restore-phased-rollouts-in-whiteout-survival)

### Inferences
Design principles drawn from the complaints:
1. Rewarded ads only, opt-in, capped, never covering UI. A paid "remove ads" must remove *all* forced ads and restore reliably across devices.
2. Monetize cosmetics and convenience, not competitive power.
3. No difficulty spikes tuned to "one move short".
4. Earnable paths for every paid item, shown visibly.
5. Social and core features (trading, PvP) must never be gated by paid currency.
6. Real-money prices shown, odds disclosed, no FOMO countdowns for minors.
